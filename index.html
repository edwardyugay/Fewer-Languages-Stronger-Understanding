01 GLOBAL IDEA

Fewer Languages, Stronger Understanding

Problem: language barriers slow real communication.

As the world becomes more interconnected, several languages disappear every year. Many people see this as a serious cultural loss, because language is connected to identity, tradition, and history. However, I strongly believe that the gradual disappearance of some languages can be a positive development. In my opinion, a world with fewer language barriers can help people communicate more easily, avoid misunderstandings, integrate into society faster, and cooperate more effectively. Although language loss may bring some cultural disadvantages, I believe that practical communication and mutual understanding should be prioritized in a global society.

02 HEALTHCARE

Doctor–Patient Language Barrier

Problem: unclear medical communication can affect safety.

First of all, fewer language barriers can make everyday life much easier. When people share a common language, they can understand one another more quickly and avoid unnecessary confusion. This is especially important in areas such as healthcare, education, business, and public services, where clear communication can directly affect people’s safety and quality of life. Al Shamsi et al. (2020) found that language barriers in healthcare can lead to miscommunication between medical professionals and patients, reduce satisfaction, and decrease the quality and safety of medical care. This shows that language barriers are not only a cultural issue, but also a practical problem that can influence real human lives.

03 WORKPLACE

Workplace Confusion

Problem: teams lose speed, ideas, and trust.

Language barriers also create serious problems in the workplace. In international companies and multicultural teams, people need to share information, solve problems, and make decisions together. If employees cannot fully understand one another, teamwork becomes slower and less efficient. Tenzer, Pudelko, and Zellmer-Bruhn (2021) found that language barriers reduce participation in team communication and make knowledge processing more difficult in multinational teams. Therefore, from a practical point of view, fewer major language barriers can support stronger cooperation, higher productivity, and better trust between people from different countries.

04 SYMBOL

The Tower of Babel

Problem: divided language can symbolize separation.

This idea can also be connected to the biblical story of the Tower of Babel. According to Genesis 11:1–9, people once had one language and common speech, but after their language was confused, they could no longer understand one another and were scattered across the earth. Of course, this story is not scientific evidence. However, it is a powerful cultural and religious example that shows how language division can symbolize confusion, separation, and distance between people. In this sense, the Tower of Babel supports the idea that a shared language can help people stay united and work together more effectively.

05 PERSONAL STORY

Losing Fluency, Finding a Future

Problem: adaptation can weaken a native language.

My personal experience also makes me support this position more strongly. I am a student from Uzbekistan, and I have lived in Korea for more than twelve years. Because I use Korean in my daily life, studies, and social communication, I have gradually started to forget some parts of my native language, Russian. This experience shows the real cost of adapting to a dominant language. However, it also shows the benefit. Korean has helped me live in Korean society, communicate with people, study effectively, and build my future. For me, the practical advantages of using a common language are stronger than the emotional difficulty of losing some fluency in my native language. Köpke and Genevska-Hanke (2018) explain that a person’s first language can become weaker when another language becomes more dominant in daily life, which supports this personal experience.

06 PUBLIC LIFE

Communication Matters Most

Problem: public safety needs shared understanding.

Therefore, I believe society should focus less on preserving every language and more on reducing communication barriers. This does not mean that people should be forced to abandon their native languages. Native languages can still be respected within families and cultural communities. However, in education, work, healthcare, aviation, and international cooperation, common languages are more practical and more useful. A language has value, but communication has a higher social priority because it directly affects safety, opportunity, and cooperation.

07 CONCLUSION

A More Connected World

Final idea: language is valuable, but understanding is essential.

In conclusion, I strongly agree that the disappearance of some languages can bring more benefits than disadvantages. While languages are important for culture and identity, language barriers often create misunderstanding, isolation, and practical difficulties. Common languages can make life easier, improve cooperation, and help people integrate into society faster. The Tower of Babel symbolically reminds us that language confusion can divide people, and modern research shows that language barriers create real problems in healthcare and multinational workplaces. Therefore, I believe that the future should focus on building a more connected world where people can understand one another through shared communication, even if this means that some languages gradually disappear.
